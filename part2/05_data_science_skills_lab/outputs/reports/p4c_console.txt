[CRISP-DM Phase 4 - Modeling]  skill: root-cause-investigation
  December 2011 revenue fell 70% month-on-month. Why?==============================================================================
  step 1 validate the change:
          Nov 2011 GBP1,461,756 -> Dec 2011 GBP433,668  (-70.3%)
          z-score vs the prior 11 months: -1.51
          -> beyond normal variance, investigate

  step 2 establish the timeline:
          December has data on 8 days; last invoice 2011-12-09 12:50:00
          November had 26 active days
          -> a hard stop mid-month, not a gradual decline. That shape points at
             collection, not demand.

  step 3 decompose the metric (revenue = orders x AOV):
          orders  3,462 -> 1,015  (-70.7%)
          AOV     GBP422.23 -> GBP427.26  (+1.2%)
          -> ORDER VOLUME is the driver. AOV is essentially unchanged, which rules
             out a pricing, mix or discounting explanation.

  step 4 drill down by dimension (Nov vs Dec):
          top Country contributors to the change:
            United Kingdom             -894,070  ( -69.7%)   87.0% of the total change
            France                      -23,649  ( -77.0%)    2.3% of the total change
            EIRE                        -23,090  ( -75.9%)    2.2% of the total change
            Germany                     -18,240  ( -70.0%)    1.8% of the total change
            Netherlands                 -14,128  ( -54.6%)    1.4% of the total change
            Japan                        -8,577  (-101.4%)    0.8% of the total change
          -> the decline is uniform across every market. A demand shock, a
             competitor, or a campaign change would hit some markets and not others.

  step 5 hypotheses, tested:
          [REJECTED] Genuine demand collapse
                     the decline is uniform across all 38 countries and AOV is flat; real demand shocks are uneven
          [REJECTED] Price or product-mix change
                     AOV moved only +1.2%; the loss is entirely order count
          [REJECTED] Seasonal (post-Christmas) drop
                     Dec 2010 was the strongest month in the series; this business peaks in Nov-Dec
          [ACCEPTED] Data extraction cut-off
                     the file ends 2011-12-09 -- December is only 9 of 31 days

  step 6 root cause: December 2011 is a PARTIAL MONTH, not a business event.
          9 of 31 days present (29% of the month)
          observed GBP433,668 -> pro-rated full-month run rate GBP1,493,745
          vs November GBP1,461,756  =  +2.2% on a like-for-like basis
          -> the 'collapse' is a reporting artefact. Recommendation: exclude partial
             periods from month-on-month charts, or label them explicitly.
