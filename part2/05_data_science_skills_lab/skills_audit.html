What the data said

Four findings that changed a decision

Online Retail · segmentation 12.9% → 62.7%

558 “Champion” accounts generate £5.6M of £8.9M in revenue. RFM plus k-means, k=4, silhouette 0.381. Retention spend not aimed here is mis-aimed.

Online Retail · funnel £718K left on the table

1,493 customers — one in three — never place a second order. The median gap to a second order is 50 days, so the intervention window is real.

Online Retail · root cause −70% → +2.2%

The December revenue “collapse” was a partial month, not a business event. The extract ends on 9 December. Pro-rated, December runs slightly ahead of November.

Titanic · model evaluation 0.417 vs 0.867 recall

Aggregate PR-AUC of 0.791 hid it: the model catches under half the male survivors it should. This blocks promotion to any automated decision.

The reconciliation that made the rest trustworthy. Two defensible definitions of “total revenue” differed by £836,340 (8.58%). Decomposed to a zero residual: cancellations −£896,812, unidentified customers +£1,733,152. Not a pipeline defect — two different questions.

The lifecycle

Where each skill was used

1

Business Understanding 2 skills

Two vague asks scoped into briefs with success criteria. Five risks logged before any data was touched — two of which later materialised exactly as written.

2

Data Understanding 10 skills

Both datasets profiled; the flat retail file normalised into a four-table SQLite star schema so the SQL-shaped skills had real relations to work on; the two revenue definitions reconciled to zero.

3

Data Preparation 2 skills

Split before cleaning; every statistic learned on train only. Target encoding cross-fitted — the in-fold version correlates 0.262 with the target versus 0.180 out-of-fold, and that difference is pure leakage.

4

Modeling 17 skills

Track A: pipelines, imbalance, Optuna, a PyTorch loop, hybrid RAG over 346 chunks, a real LoRA fine-tune. Track B: cohorts, RFM segments, funnel, ARIMA, structured root-cause, A/B statistics.

5

Evaluation 9 skills

Findings converted to ranked insights, impact quantified as a range, jargon translated from grade 14.3 to 8.7, and a QA gate that returned one FAIL — blocking automated use.

6

Deployment 6 skills

FastAPI service over the whole pipeline (p50 14ms, batching 180× cheaper per row), plus the executive summary, dashboard spec, catalog entry and retrospective.

What broke

Sixteen of 36 bundled skill scripts did not run

All are fixed by unified diffs in patches/. The first row is the serious one: those scripts parse their arguments, ignore them, and print results computed from a hardcoded demo. A tool that silently answers about the wrong data is worse than no tool.

DefectFilesEffect when runSeverity
__main__ runs a demo instead of calling main() 11 segmentation_runner.py reported “LTV by PLAN, N=20” for a 4,338-customer segmentation; catalog_extractor.py returned a fictional orders_fact table for every database given to it. critical
Bare % in an argparse help string 3 Hard crash on Python 3.14: ValueError: badly formed help string. Fails loudly, so at least it cannot mislead. blocking
.dt.to_period("MS") — MS is a DateOffset, not a Period frequency 1 cohort_builder.py raises, taking the whole cohort chain down with it. blocking
sort_values on a column-less empty frame 1 KeyError whenever no correlation pair clears the threshold — i.e. exactly when the data is clean. blocking
Why these survived. Both repos ship validators, and both check documentation structure rather than script execution. The guidance in all 46 skills is sound; the tooling in 16 of them was not. A skill’s prose and a skill’s tooling are separate products and need separate review.
